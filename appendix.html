01 · Methodology

How this got built, honestly.

Four layers, in order. Nothing here is a single ChatGPT prompt dressed up as diligence.

Layer 1 · The dossier

A 1,145-line OSINT pack on Pureplay Skin Sciences: MCA-derived corporate records, the full Delhi HC docket, funding chronology, trademark history, and six of Shankar's long-form interviews transcribed and cross-checked across 85 individually graded sources (statutory record vs. company claim vs. founder statement vs. press vs. anecdote, kept separate throughout).

Layer 2 · Adversarial deep research, run one

A structured prompt built specifically to try to refute a hypothesis I had, not confirm it: "commodity actives are easier to counterfeit, and that's why Plum got hit." Full verdict below. It didn't survive.

Layer 3 · Adversarial deep research, run two

A second run scanning Korean, Japanese and Chinese beauty markets for ingredient and format whitespace, filtered against real Indian demand signal (Reddit, marketplace reviews, pricing) rather than global trend-deck noise. Six candidates went in. Two survived contact with Plum's actual catalog.

Layer 4 · Manual spot-checks

Before anything from the deep-research runs went into the two-pager, I independently re-verified the load-bearing claims: Anua's live Nykaa listing, the Olive Young 2025 awards badge, SEEN Group's existence as a real London beauty PR firm, KorinMi's funding and founder, and Plum's own current toner/pad catalog for overlap.

02 · The autopsy

The hypothesis I killed before you could.

The original instinct: Plum's hero actives (vitamin C, niacinamide) are commodity ingredients, which makes them structurally easier to counterfeit, and a novel active would be a real anti-counterfeiting strategy. I didn't like how convenient that sounded, so I ran it adversarially before it went anywhere near a brief with your name on it.

Hypothesis statusDEAD
Partially true, but overstated. For your specific litigation, the wrong explanation.
Confidence: high, ≈85%. Full reasoning ran across OECD/EUIPO customs-seizure research, Korean and Indian enforcement actions, a Which? marketplace investigation, and a direct re-read of the CS(COMM) 1139/2025 order.
Claim testedVerdictWhy
Plum's hero SKUs use widely available activesStandsFactually correct, not in dispute
Commodity actives → easier functional imitationStands, narrowlyTrue at the margin for high-fidelity fakes, not the dominant driver
Commodity actives → more counterfeiting overallDoesn't holdNo comparative dataset supports it; confounded by popularity, price, channel
Your actual court case is formula counterfeitingDoesn't holdPlum side is expired-stock date tampering (¶25.7); Phy side unresolved on mechanism
Proprietary formulas (SK-II, La Mer) resist counterfeitingContradictedBoth faked at industrial scale; contents didn't match, packaging fooled distributors anyway
A novel active would meaningfully reduce your exposureNot supportedReal defenses are reverse-logistics reconciliation, serial tracking, tamper-evident coding
What this means for the brief: the litigation doesn't appear in it. Not because it's not real, but because forcing a causal link that doesn't survive scrutiny is a worse look than leaving it out. Good thing we checked first.
03 · The full whitespace scan

Six candidates in. Two survived.

The two-pager only shows you the winner. Here's everything else that got tested and why it got cut, so you know this wasn't a coin flip.

PDRN / polynucleotidesclaimedDr. Sheth's already live on Nykaa. Window closed within months of Seoul going mainstream on it.
Exosome serumsclaimedDr. Sheth's and ClayCo both in-market. Claims substantiation is also genuinely harder to communicate cleanly than heartleaf.
Spicule / microneedle serumsclaimedThe Derma Co and ClayCo already own the domestic "microneedle serum" positioning. Long-term safety data is also thinner than for topical formats.
Overnight wrapping masksclaimedBiodance proved the demand (thousands of Nykaa ratings); Dr. Sheth's copied it domestically in June 2026. Window's gone.
Chinese recombinant collagenwatch, not launchMassive scale in China (Giant Biogene / Comfy), but no meaningful Indian imported-demand pull yet, and an active labeling-claims dispute in its home market. Too early here.
Fermented bean / soybean essencedemand too thinMixsoon is on Nykaa, but ratings are still in the tens, and there's no Olive Young top-2 category placement behind it like heartleaf has. Real, just not proven enough yet.

One more that didn't make the two-pager for a different reason, not because it's weak, because it's already crowded: a Bioré-style watery Japanese UV essence. Real Indian appetite for the texture is well documented on Reddit and in Nykaa reviews, but Plum, Minimalist, Dot & Key and Foxtale are already fighting over "lightweight, no white cast" sunscreen positioning. It's a genuinely good R&D brief. It's just not a new word in the way heartleaf is.
